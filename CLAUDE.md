# WRECKED — briefing for Claude

You are continuing work on **WRECKED**, a multiplayer VR ape brawler for Meta Quest, with the game's creator. Read this before doing anything.

## How to work with the creator (most important)

- The creator gives **strategic, verbal-level direction** (often dictated). They do **not** want technical tasks. Do the work yourself: edit files, run Unity checks, commit and push. Never ask them to download, upload, install, copy files or paste logs if you can do it yourself.
- Their only regular jobs: press **Play** in Unity (or put on the headset) and say how it feels. Ask for that in one short, plain sentence.
- Explain results in plain language, short. No jargon. When something needs a decision, give a recommendation, not a menu.
- Keep them in the loop on what changed in-game, not on code details.

## Checking Unity yourself

- While the Editor is open, Unity 6.6 writes its log to the project: `WRECKED-Unity\Logs\Editor.log` (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Unity\Editor\Editor.log` only has the first few startup lines). Read it for compile errors and Debug.Log output instead of asking the creator to copy errors.
- To open Unity straight into Play mode yourself: `Unity.exe -projectPath WRECKED-Unity -executeMethod Wrecked.EditorTools.WreckedSetup.PlayMain` (also on the menu as WRECKED → Play Main Scene). Without a headset, the `XR_ERROR_RUNTIME_UNAVAILABLE` lines in the log are expected; the game falls back to desktop mode.
- Headless setup also works: add `-batchmode -quit -executeMethod Wrecked.EditorTools.WreckedSetup.SetUp -logFile <file>` (Editor must not have this project open).
- After editing scripts, Unity recompiles when its window regains focus; if needed, ask the creator to click into Unity (one sentence), then read the log.
- With the Editor closed you can compile-check headless: `"<Unity.exe path>" -batchmode -nographics -quit -projectPath WRECKED-Unity -logFile -` (find Unity.exe under `C:\Program Files\Unity\Hub\Editor\<version>\Editor\`).
- Building to the Quest (needs developer mode + USB): `File → Build Profiles → Android → Build And Run`; can also be scripted with `-executeMethod` later.

## Game design (current)

- **Core:** Gorilla-Tag-style arm locomotion (push the world, fling with a 0.1 s velocity history, cap 7.5 m/s), hold grip to hang (grip resists light hits < 5.5 m/s). **Grip stamina** (`GRIP`, `gripTick`): one shared timer for both hands counts while any hand hangs on a surface with grip (tools, bodies and apes held never count; no wrist text or bar for it: the creator wants only the buzz); letting go in mid-air or switching hands keeps the time; from 10 s the gripping controllers buzz harder; at 15 s you are made to let go and grip can't hang on again until you have stood on the ground for 1 s (which refills it); a respawn refills it too, grab and ride the swinging log and ropes.
- **Fighting (Gang Beasts-style):** slap with hand speed (min 2.2 m/s), dizzy builds 3.2 per m/s of slap speed, dizzy at 100 → 3.5 s knocked out (shake arms to wake early). Grab with grip; throws are 0.9x hand speed (cap 7, awake targets 0.6x). Grabbed apes escape by slapping the holder: 3 slaps (hard ones count double) or knocking them dizzy. Bots slap back when grabbed.
- **Rounds:** free-for-all, 6 apes, last ape standing; 120 s; fall below y = -8 → out (no grabbing), but you keep falling until you splash into the river (`WATER_Y`), then go to the out ledge; fallen bots also fall to the river and vanish. No scoreboard in the arena during fights: when a round ends the scoreboard floats in front of each player for the ~5.5 s before the next round (depthTest must stay on, it vanished with depthTest off). The Yard keeps its scoreboard on the cabin roof.
- **Mirror:** a fixed camera behind the glass draws the Yard onto a screen every 2nd frame (`renderMirror`; proven in the headset, cheap). Your own ape is on `SELF_LAYER` (only the mirror and shadow cameras see it) and is drawn the way others see you: hands in line with the arms (`selfIM.hands`, `FS_SELF` fingers); your first-person hands (`myHandsIM`, `FS_MINE`), which follow the controller's own angle, and the hit vignette are hidden while the mirror draws. A three.js `Reflector` was tried and removed: it can't be tested without a headset, costs two extra scene renders per frame in VR, and calling `getWorldPosition()` on an XR eye camera corrupts its matrix. Canyon-wall crystals are raycast onto the cliff faces at load.
- **Map:** Ravine Sawmill — 14×14 m main deck on a tall braced trestle over a ravine, swinging log on an A-framed frame (braces are climbable), saw house on the east deck (back wall, spinning saw blade at `SAW`: touching it in a fight throws you back, +28 dizzy, bleeding), water wheel (`WHEEL`) fed by a flume from the canyon wall, river at the bottom of the ravine and a big waterfall at the north end (animated water in `arenaTick`), log bridge to a crystal rock island, lookout with a flag. Decorations in `buildArenaDecor()`. Pixel-texture look (low-res wood/rock/moss, glowing crystals) per the creator's reference images; original chimp-like apes, not Gorilla Tag's art.
- **No main menu:** the page loads straight into the Yard and joins Quick Play online. Browsers only start VR from a button press, so one Enter VR button sits over the game (on a computer: click to play). Name, room code and settings live on the Yard computer.
- **The Yard (lobby)** is a backyard: log cabin on the north side (mirror on its porch wall, scoreboard on the roof), picket fence plus an invisible barrier (17 m walls just outside it and a lid at +16 m) so nobody can leave the Yard, big tree with treehouse (NW; the creator asked for the tire swing to be removed), campfire with log benches in the middle where everyone spawns, picnic table, log pile, computer booth (W), START pad (E), shop stall and tool rack (S), lamp posts and string lights, and a **Join the Discord** sign (NE, right of the cabin: `DISC_SIGN`, `DISC_BTN`, `openDiscord`). Slap its blue button in VR (the VR session ends, because a page cannot open a tab from inside VR, and a big "Join the WRECKED Discord" link shows on the screen) or click it or the sign on PC (opens https://discord.gg/jKxcWaZRgG in a new tab). Decorations live in `buildYardDecor()`; solid parts are boxes in `buildLevel()`.
- **Ape model (creator's Meshy AI model):** `models/ape.glb` (web copy: 8.4k triangles, Unity Humanoid rig, 1024 texture whose alpha marks fur so each player's colour replaces the blue; tangents and animations stripped). The creator's original file (with Walking/Running animations) is `WRECKED-Unity/Assets/Models/WreckedApe_Meshy_UnityHumanoid.glb` for the Unity version (needs a glTF importer such as glTFast). In the web game `RIG` / `skinPose()` pose a copy per ape every frame: arms reach the hands with two-bone IK (stretching when needed), the model's own hands are tucked away (the game draws its own hands with fingers), and **no legs** (creator's choice, Gorilla Tag style: leg bones are folded into the hips). `scene.matrixWorldAutoUpdate` is off; the loop calls `scene.updateMatrixWorld()` then `applySkins()` before rendering. Head items use `HEADMAP` (fitted to the model's head); the model has no headband. Meshy's head mesh has holes and ~330 backwards triangles: normals are used as stored (no gl_FrontFacing flip), a fur cap (`headCapGeo`) covers the back and top of the head, and the texture bake fills the gaps between UV islands and forces head/body triangles to fur. The built-in code ape (below) is the fallback if the model fails to load.
- **Prop models from Meshy (`loadProps()`):** Meshy exports are huge (crate 715k triangles / 25 MB, hammer 88k / 4 MB): simplify with gltf-transform + meshoptimizer and shrink textures to 512 before use. The crate is drawn as a box wearing flat renders of the Meshy crate's faces (`models/crate.jpg`: sides left half, top right half) because a crate is just a box; tools (`models/{mallet,pan,plunger,chicken}.glb`, ~2.5k triangles each) are pre-baked into the hand frame (grip at the origin, pointing along -z, tool-sized) so the loader uses them as-is; The launcher is two Meshy models: `launcher.glb` (its built-in red tip cut off; held by the back handle) and `glove.glb`; the glove sits loaded in the barrel (`GLOVE_REST`, `loadGlove`) and is what flies out on a coiled spring (`Helix` tube stretched by `setSeg`). Meshy files carry huge mesh "extras" metadata: strip it (`setExtras({})`). Still to come from the creator: knife, banana peel. Crystal clusters everywhere are the creator's four Meshy crystals (`models/crystal-{blue,pink,green,red}.glb`, normal/roughness maps stripped), placed at `CRYSTAL_SPOTS` by `loadCrystals()` with a glowing, gently pulsing `crystalMat`. Low-poly copies for Unity are in `WRECKED-Unity/Assets/Models/`. Built-in shapes stay as fallbacks.
- **Ape model (web, built in code):** `APE_PARTS` (broad chest, lighter belly, shoulder bumps, hair tuft, heart-shaped face, muzzle, chin, heavy brow, ears, eyes with glints, headband on the forehead), a separate mouth that opens with the voice, and limbs drawn by `drawLimbs()`: two-part arms that bend at the elbow (`elbowOf`) and short legs with feet that waddle with walking speed. The creator may make a model in Meshy AI later: it needs to be rigged (humanoid skeleton), low-poly for Quest, exported as .glb; arms are then driven by the controllers with IK.
- **Knife (nerfed):** 3 uses, weak dizziness (k 0.45), it only wrecks a dizzy ape with a strong stab (speed ≥ 3.5); a fresh ape takes ~8 stabs, i.e. more than one knife. It's a finisher, not a one-hit kill.
- **Face wounds:** weapon hits (not bare hands or the banana peel) add a bleeding wound to the victim's head (`addWound`, up to 6, biased to the face, some drip), drawn as an InstancedMesh decal (`woundIM`, blood shader) on the head ellipsoid via HEADMAP; synced with `EV.FX` k 'wound'; cleared on respawn (`resetActor`).
- **Scrap (the money) + shop (web):** the currency is called **scrap** everywhere players see it (cog icon in `coinIcon`); the code still stores it as `wallet.coins`. `wallet` in localStorage (coins, owned, wear {head, face}, daily-bonus day); new players start with 500. Earned only in matches: +1 slap, +10 dizzy, +15 knock-off (last hitter within 8 s), +25 wreck, +10 per round played, +20 round win, +50 match win, +100 daily bonus. Credit goes to the last hitter: the game that owns the victim sends `EV.CREDIT` to the attacker's owner. Shop room (`SR`, 220 m west of the Yard; 12×12 m, roof and rafters that don't cast shadows, rug, string lights, hanging lamps, shelves, plants): nothing of the shop is in the Yard any more. You get there from the **hand menu** (`MENU`): **Y on the left controller** (buttons[5]; the left ≡ button is kept by the Quest browser in WebXR, any extra left button index ≥ 6 also opens it; use the real menu button in the Unity version) or **M** on PC. The menu pops up over your left hand (touch buttons with the right hand; on PC it stands in front of you, click it): SHOP / BACK TO THE YARD (Yard only), MIC ON/OFF, LEAVE ROOM (private code → Quick Play), LEAVE GAME (ends VR; CLOSE MENU on PC). Players in the same room who go to the shop see each other there (it's the same world). AFK players stay in the shop through a match; bots never chase anyone in it. **You arrive on a little street** (`SHOP_SPAWN`, on the sidewalk facing the shop): sidewalks with curbs, a road with a crossing to the shop door, a brick building and a building with a closed shutter either side of the shop, a row of buildings across the road, planter trees, and the creator's Meshy street props (`STREET_PROPS`, `models/street-*.glb`, shrunk with tools/prop.mjs to ~2.4-4.4k tris and 512 jpg each): 3 lantern street lamps (`STREET_LAMPS`, lanterns at `LANTERN_Y`, glowing halo sprites), a bench, a fire hydrant, an overflowing bin and a mailbox, all solid (hidden colliders), road barriers closing both ends; invisible barriers keep everyone in: 1.2 m thick walls round the street (both ends, behind the shop, behind the far houses) up to +15, a ceiling at +6.4 (just under the lowest house roof, so nobody stands on the houses), the space over the shop filled in from +4.4 (no standing on its roof) and a spare lid at +14; tested with flings at the game's top speeds (~9 m/s) (`inShopArea()` covers the shop and street). The shop front has a doorway and **two big windows** (solid glass: hidden colliders, a see-through pane with shine streaks), striped awnings and cream frames. No sign outside: the creator's **WRECK SHOP poster** (`models/poster.glb`: Meshy relief flattened to paper with tools/poster.mjs, 5.7k tris, 1024 jpg) is stuck on the outside of the left window with **clear tape on each corner** (`POSTER.tape`, canvas strips with torn ends), with plain paper backing seen from inside. The mirror is on the left wall by the door. **It's always night on the street** (only for whoever is there: `SUN.nightK` = 1 while `inShop()`): `sunLight()` in every lit shader switches to cool moonlight with warm pools under the 3 street lamps and the door lamp (`SUN.lampP`, the lit spot comes from a global `gW` set in `sunShadow`), while anything inside the shop's walls (`SUN.shopC` box) stays warm and bright; the sky gets stars and a moon, the haze goes dark (`setNightFog`), and some street windows glow (`winGlowIM`, from `STREET_WINS`). Inside: each item on its own pedestal (`SHOP_PEDS`, 6 per side wall) worn by a **real ape head** (`buildBusts()`: the model's head triangles, weight > 0.5 on Head/head_end/headfront, taken through the bind matrix, plus the head cap; items placed with HEADMAP) with a tier ring. **Buying works like other ape games: no screen, every pedestal has its own BUY button** (`PED_BTNS`, a dome on the pedestal front under the label; touch it in VR, click it on PC → `buyItem(i)`): green = buy, orange = not enough scrap, blue = wear, red = take off, grey = sold out. The label (`drawPedLabel`) shows the tier or a red **LIMITED · ENDS IN …** ribbon, name, price or OWNED/WEARING, and what the button does or a short message. **Limited items** (`LIMITED` by name → end date) can't be bought after their date (owners keep them). `LIMITED` is empty for now: the creator wants limited items only once the game is out and has players, to make them worth more; it uses the device clock, so the store version needs the server to say what's on sale. **Try on:** touching a head (clicking it on PC) puts it under the spotlight and on the display ape, and you see yourself wearing it in the mirror (`mirrorCos()`; only you, only in the shop). The back wall has 3 stocked shelves and the counter with the till. The Yard's mirror camera moves onto the shop's front wall while you're in there (`placeMirror`). 12 cosmetics in `COS` (8 hats, 4 face items; 500 / 1,500 / 4,000), worn by everyone via `wearCos`, synced in the LOOK event (`w`). Coins are device-local for now: the store version needs server-side saving (Meta account / backend) so coins can't be faked or lost.
- **Voice chat (web):** proximity voice over WebRTC, peer-to-peer between everyone in the Photon room; Photon events (`EV.RTC`) carry offers/answers/ICE. The earlier member calls each newcomer. Voices are 3D (WebAudio HRTF panner at each player's ape, fading with distance); every other ape has a floating name tag with a speaker icon (dark = quiet, bright with waves = talking, red cross = muted), and mouths open with the voice level (`mouthIM`, also your own ape in the mirror). Mic is asked for on the first click (PC) or on Enter VR (waits up to 4 s for the permission prompt). Computer VOICE tab: M = mic on/off, 1-5 = mute a player. Only public STUN servers are used, so a few strict networks may fail to connect; a TURN server would fix that. In Unity this becomes Photon Voice. Needs mute/report moderation before the store.
- **Lobby flow (creator's choice, like Gilla):** no start button. The Yard runs a 20-second timer (`LOBBY_WAIT`, state 'starting'); when it ends everyone who isn't AFK goes into the match (if nobody is ready, it loops). The old START pad is gone; the button by the sign is the **AFK button** (slap it in VR; Enter or click on PC): AFK players get no seat (`buildMap(true)` skips them), stay in the Yard during matches (`human.afkYard`, `yardHere()`), and pressing it again puts them in from the next round. AFK state is sent in the LOOK event (`k`).
- **Ragdolls (`ragStep`, visual only):** dizzy apes, apes held while dizzy, thrown apes (for the whole flight, until they land) and wrecked bodies go floppy. The real position keeps its normal physics; the drawn body is laid flat on the floor found by a raycast straight down (`lie` = 0.2 m above it; for remote players up to 2 m below their head counts as the floor), tumbles in the air from its velocity, or dangles from the holding hand. Arms are verlet chains from the shoulders (0.2-0.58 m) pushed out of the level, so they rest on the floor. Grabs, slaps and tool hits use `reachD()` (nearest of the real position and the floppy body). Thrown is sent to others as flag 64. Carried bodies are pushed out of the floor so they can't sink through it.
- **Robustness rules (from the bug hunt):**
  - `wake()` / `autoAfk()`: a page that has only been opened (no click, key or Enter VR yet), a hidden tab, or a headset that's been taken off (`XRSession` visibilitychange) counts as AFK, so idle players aren't seated in matches. The host treats players it hasn't had a LOOK from as AFK.
  - Host hand-over: a host whose page/session goes hidden calls `passHost()` (`room.setMasterClient`); if a host goes silent for 5 s of a client's own running time, the lowest-numbered other player takes over (`hostWatch`). The shared clock for the swinging log survives a host change via `net.logOff`. These use the real Photon server and are NOT covered by the mock tests.
  - Auto-reconnect after a dropped connection (up to 5 tries). `PHOTON_VERSION` must be bumped whenever network messages change (now `wrecked-1.1`) so stale cached pages can't join newer ones.
  - `placeHuman()` always clears `noGrab` / `fallT`; a VR hand whose controller loses tracking un-anchors; falling off drops your tools; the host drops tools whose holder left/fell; item ids stay unique after a host change; `pendingDrop` stops a thrown tool flicking back to your hand.
  - Anything that must be visible in VR goes on layer 0: three r160's XR cameras only draw layers 0-2 (the hit vignette was invisible in VR on layer 7).
  - If VR runs under 50 fps for ~8 s, shadows switch themselves off (`perfWatch`).
  - **Guests:** players with no seat in the running match (AFK in the Yard, or a mid-match joiner on the out ledge) send `EV.GPOSE` (keyed by Photon actor number, 10/s) instead of POSE. Everyone shows them from `guestPool` (6 spare proxies, `i` = 10-15, `guest: true`): drawn, name-tagged, voice comes from them, mouths move. They are deliberately NOT in `actors`, so they can't be slapped, grabbed or use tools until they have a seat; they vanish when seated again, on leaving, or after 2.5 s of silence. `MAXA` (max apes drawn) is 12 for this.
  - Never put code after a `//` comment on the same line (one such line silently disabled dizzy stars and other apes' palms).
- **Yard computer** (lobby, west desk): green-screen terminal with a full keyboard you tap with your hands (PC: look at a key and click). Tabs: ROOM (join by code / Quick Play), NAME, VOICE, COLOR (fur colour, R/G/B 0-9, synced to others with the LOOK event), CODE (promo codes unlock fur skins: WRECKED, BANANA, GHOST, GOLDAPE; add new ones in `CODES`), OPTIONS (blood, shadows).
- **Blood:** liquid look: glossy stretched droplets, wet shiny pools/splatters, drips running down walls, wrecked bodies bleed into a growing pool. Blood can be switched off.
- **Graphics:** real-time sun shadows (a depth map around the player; can be switched off on the computer if a headset struggles), warm sun / cool shade lighting. Fonts: Rubik Distressed (titles), Rubik Wet Paint (logo), Barlow Condensed (text), VT323 (computer).
- The creator can make 3D models; load them as .glb (three.js GLTFLoader) when they send some.
- Name: **WRECKED** (repo is still called `ape-rumble`).
